Status · Founding Phase

The Continuity Bureau is in its founding phase. It is not, at this time, a registered political party in any state of the United States. It is not, at this time, a registered political party with Elections Canada or any provincial elections authority. No candidates. No ballot lines. No electoral track record. Registration in relevant jurisdictions is administrative work in progress, and it will take the time honest paperwork takes.

We state this plainly because implying official standing we do not have is precisely the species of lie this movement exists to make unaffordable. Our claim, today, proofs out to founding phase, building in public, papers pending.

The Twelve Principles

  1. The individual is the base layer.

    Every family, firm, church, commune, city, and state is an abstraction built on top of individuals. Nothing built on the base layer outranks it.

  2. Every centralizing abstraction must justify its existence continuously — or be forked.

    Authority is a service with an uptime requirement, and "we have always been in charge" is not a status page.

  3. Verify, don't trust.

    Institutions earn exactly the authority their proofs support. No proofs, no authority.

  4. Subsidiarity is not a preference; it is an engineering principle.

    Every decision is made at the smallest scale competent to make it. Escalation requires justification. De-escalation does not.

  5. Voluntary association is the only legitimate membership.

    Any affiliation you cannot leave is not a community. It is a containment structure with a newsletter.

  6. No walled gardens.

    Not in software, not in money, not in speech, not in governance. A garden you cannot exit intact was never a garden.

  7. Exit outranks voice.

    Voice is the right to complain to management. Exit is the right to stop having management. We support both.

  8. Open source is civic virtue.

    A rule you cannot read is not a rule; it is an ambush. Closed systems governing public life are guilty until audited.

  9. Responsibility to the responsible.

    Respect, authority, and trust are extended to those who demonstrate them — individually, by evidence, without regard to title or incumbency.

  10. Build > protest.

    A protest asks the incumbent to change. A working alternative makes the incumbent irrelevant. One of these has a track record.

  11. Competence is the only aristocracy we acknowledge.

    Not hereditary, not credentialed by default, not transferable. Demonstrated per task, forever.

  12. Entropy is on our side.

    We do not have to defeat centralized systems. We have to outlast them. This is not patience. It is thermodynamics.

Builds / Refuses

What the Bureau builds

  • Reference implementations of self-sovereignty. Identity you hold, communication that cannot be silently intercepted, records that survive any single company or agency.
  • Verification tooling for public institutions. Budgets, voting records, and contract awards, inspectable in machine-readable form. Sunlight as a service.
  • Subsidiarity kits. Playbooks and software for communities re-onshoring their own competence: mutual aid, dispute resolution, resilient utilities.
  • Civic literacy for the verification age. How to read a primary source, check a signature, and notice when you are being asked to trust instead of shown.
  • The administrative machinery of a real political movement, built in the open, at the pace honest paperwork actually takes.

Everything ships under open licenses. If the Bureau vanished tomorrow, the tools would keep running. That is the acceptance criterion.

What the Bureau refuses

  • Violence — actual, threatened, or winked at. Entropy is already on our side. We build while waiting.
  • Hatred and dehumanization of any group. Our contempt is reserved for systems and architectures, and it is fully allocated there.
  • Cults of personality — including around our own founder. Everything here is forkable, and that includes the leadership.
  • Election disinformation and fabricated claims. A verification movement gets exactly one credibility, non-refundable.
  • Attacks on named individuals. Institutions are fair game. Persons are not targets.
  • Coercive recruitment and retention. If our arguments require a sales funnel, they are not arguments.
  • Token and coin promotion. Decentralization is an architecture, not a ticker symbol.
